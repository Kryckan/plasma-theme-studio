Plasma Theme Studio

One app for editing a whole Plasma theme in one place. You set colors, corner radii, borders and shadows once. The app then writes the right files for Kvantum, Klassy, the Plasma style and GTK, and reloads the desktop live. The mockup below is interactive: drag the sliders on the right.

Plasma, Kvantum and Aurorae all draw their frames as 9-slice SVG pieces with fixed element IDs. The editor generates those pieces from the sliders, and the dashed lines show the slices. To draw freely, switch to the node tool or open the file in Inkscape.

How the pieces fit

One theme file, many generators

The app does not replace Kvantum, Klassy or Plasma. It drives them. Everything you choose is saved in a single theme file, and each generator translates it into the format its target understands. Three of the targets draw their frames with SVG, so one SVG editor can shape apps, the panel and popups alike.

Technology

Framework, language and packaging

Framework Qt 6 Widgets
  • The same Qt as Plasma. The preview can load the real Kvantum or Klassy style and draw genuine buttons and menus.
  • QtSvg and QGraphicsView are enough for the built-in SVG editor.
  • Freehand drawing: SVG-Edit (MIT) runs inside QtWebEngine. Inkscape is available as an external button.

Not Tauri or Electron: a web view cannot render real Qt styles, so the preview would only approximate them.

Language Python + PySide6
  • Fast to build and change. The generators are mostly text and SVG templates, which suits Python well.
  • Uses the distro's pyside6 package, not pip. Otherwise the app cannot load KDE's style plugins.
  • Performance-critical parts can move to C++ later if needed.

Alternative: C++ with Qt gives the most performance and suits an official KDE project, but development is slower.

Packaging PKGBUILD (AUR)
  • The app writes to ~/.config and ~/.local/share, talks to KWin and plasmashell over D-Bus, and loads the system's Qt plugins.
  • A native package does all of that without workarounds. Other distros can follow with their own packages.

Not Flatpak or AppImage: the sandbox and the bundled Qt would hide Klassy and Kvantum from the preview.

Reused projects Drive them, don't fork them
  • Kvantum and Klassy are installed as dependencies. The app only writes their themes and settings.
  • Breeze's Plasma SVGs and Breeze GTK's CSS serve as templates.
  • All of them are GPL, so the app is GPL-3.0-or-later.

Result: when Kvantum or Klassy improve, the app benefits automatically.

Roadmap
